Selling online does not change the rules — it just changes the storefront. If you are buying cars to resell for profit, you generally need a **dealer license** whether you sell them on a lot or on an online marketplace. In fact, online listings are exactly where states look for unlicensed sellers. Here is how the rules apply to online selling.

## Quick answer

Yes — if you are selling cars for profit online at any real volume, you likely need a dealer license. The platform (a marketplace, social media, or your own site) does not exempt you. Personal, occasional sales of your own vehicles are different.

## The platform doesn't matter — the activity does

States define a "dealer" by **activity and intent**, not by where the car is listed. Buying vehicles to resell for profit is dealing, whether the sale happens on a gravel lot or a marketplace app. Online selling gives you reach, not a loophole.

If anything, online is **riskier** to do unlicensed, because that is exactly where investigators watch for the [curbstoning](/blog/how-to-get-independent-dealer-license) pattern: one seller or phone number tied to many listings.

## When you're fine without a license

- Selling **your own** vehicle that was titled in your name and you actually used.
- The occasional genuine personal sale under your state's threshold.

## When you need one

- Buying cars specifically to **flip** them online for profit.
- Listing vehicle after vehicle in a repeating pattern.
- Crossing your state's annual sales threshold.

## How to sell online legally

1. Get [licensed](/blog/how-to-get-independent-dealer-license) if your activity qualifies.
2. Keep clean records for every sale — title, odometer, tax, temp tag.
3. Post honest listings with real photos and disclosed issues.
4. Handle sales tax and titling correctly, even for remote buyers.

## A real example

Lena starts flipping cars she buys at auction, listing them on a marketplace with her cell number on each. Sales are great — until an investigator notices the same number on eight listings. Because she is unlicensed, it becomes a curbstoning problem. Had she gotten licensed first, the same online business would have been completely legitimate.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Thinking online = exempt.** It is not.
- **Reusing one phone number** across many listings.
- **Skipping sales tax or titling** on remote sales.

## Tips

- If profit is the point, get licensed before you scale online listings.
- Confirm your state's rule for out-of-state online buyers.
- Keep the same clean paperwork you would for an in-person sale.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I sell cars on a marketplace without a license?

You can sell your own personal vehicle. Selling cars for profit at volume generally requires a dealer license, online or not.

### Is online selling more likely to get flagged?

Yes — online listings are the main place states detect unlicensed dealing.

### Do I still collect sales tax on online sales?

Generally yes, with specific rules for out-of-state buyers. Confirm with your state.

## Conclusion

The internet is a storefront, not a loophole. If you are buying cars to resell for profit online, the same dealer rules apply — and online is where unlicensed sellers get caught fastest. Get licensed, keep clean paperwork, and you can build a legitimate online car business with none of the curbstoning risk.
