Online sales only alter the storefront; they do not alter the regulations. Regardless of whether you sell cars on a lot or online, you typically need a **dealer license** if you are purchasing cars to resell for a profit. In actuality, states specifically search online listings for unregistered sellers. The regulations for online sales are as follows.

## Brief response

Yes, you probably need a dealer license if you are selling cars for profit online at any significant volume. You are not exempt by the platform (a marketplace, social media, or your own website). Personal, infrequent sales of your own cars are not the same.

## The activity is what matters, not the platform.

States define a "dealer" based on **activity and intent** rather than the location of the vehicle's listing. Whether the sale takes place on a marketplace app or a gravel lot, purchasing cars to resell for a profit is considered dealing. Selling online increases your reach rather than creating a loophole.

If anything, it's **riskier** to operate unlicensed online because that's precisely where investigators look for the [curbstoning](/blog/how-to-get-independent-dealer-license) pattern: a single seller or phone number connected to numerous listings.

## When you don't need a license

Selling a car that you used and that was registered in your name.
The sporadic real personal sale that falls below the threshold of your state.

## When you require one

Purchasing vehicles with the express purpose of **flipping** them online for financial gain.
Vehicle after vehicle is listed in a pattern that repeats.
Reaching the annual sales threshold set by your state.

## How to lawfully sell online

1. If your activity meets the requirements, get [licensed](/blog/how-to-get-independent-dealer-license).
2. Maintain accurate records for each sale, including the title, odometer, tax, and temporary tag.
3. Post truthful listings with genuine images and issues that are disclosed.
4. Accurately handle titling and sales tax, even for distant buyers.

## An actual instance

Lena begins listing the cars she purchases at auction on a marketplace along with her cell phone number. Sales are excellent until an investigator discovers that eight listings have the same number. It becomes a curbstoning issue because she is not licensed. The same online business would have been totally legitimate if she had obtained a license first.

## Common errors to steer clear of

**Thinking on the internet is exempt.** It isn't.
One phone number is being used in multiple listings.
For remote sales, there is no sales tax or titling.

## Advice

Obtain a license before expanding your online listings if making money is your main goal.
Verify your state's regulations regarding out-of-state online purchasers.
Maintain the same tidy documentation as you would for a face-to-face transaction.

## Common inquiries

### Do I need a license to sell cars on a marketplace?

You are able to sell your own car. Whether online or not, a dealer license is typically needed to sell cars for profit in large quantities.

### Is it more likely that an online sale will be reported?

Yes, states find unlicensed dealing primarily through online listings.

### Do online sales still qualify for sales tax collection?

In general, yes, but there are particular guidelines for purchasers from outside the state. Verify with your state.

## Conclusion

The internet is not a loophole, but rather a storefront. The same dealer regulations apply if you purchase cars online to resell for a profit, and unlicensed sellers are most likely to be apprehended online. You can establish a legitimate online car business without the curbstoning risk if you obtain a license and maintain accurate documentation.
