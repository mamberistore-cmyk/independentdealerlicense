Choosing your lot is the decision that trips up more new dealers than any other. The perfect-looking corner is worthless if it is not **zoned** for vehicle sales, and a compliant but hidden lot bleeds money on rent. Here is how to pick a location that is both legal and profitable — starting with one free phone call.

## Quick answer

Before you sign anything, call your **local zoning office** and confirm the address allows used-vehicle sales. Then weigh lot size, signage rules, visibility, and rent against your budget. Legal first, profitable second — in that order.

## Step 1: Confirm zoning (the free call that saves thousands)

This is non-negotiable and it is free. Call your city or county zoning office and ask specifically whether the address permits **used motor vehicle sales**. Retail zoning is not the same as automotive zoning. Skipping this call is how dealers end up paying rent on a lot they legally cannot use — one of the [most common rejection reasons](/blog/why-dealer-license-applications-get-rejected).

## Step 2: Meet the state's location rules

States typically want a **real, verifiable business location**, which may include:

- A minimum lot size or number of display spaces.
- A permanent building or office.
- Permanent **signage** to a minimum spec.
- Posted business hours and a lockable records area.

Confirm these against your state's dealer-premises checklist before you commit.

## Step 3: Weigh profitability

Once a site is legal, judge it as a business:

- **Visibility and traffic** — a busy road is free marketing.
- **Rent vs. budget** — location is often the biggest monthly cost.
- **Room to grow** — enough display space for your inventory plan.

Balance it against your [startup budget](/blog/how-much-does-an-independent-dealer-license-cost); the cheapest lot is not cheap if it cannot move cars.

## A real example

Jordan finds a great-looking lot on a busy road at a low rent. Before signing, he calls zoning — and learns the parcel is retail-but-not-automotive. He walks away and finds a compliant site nearby. His neighbor Maria skipped the call, signed a year lease, and got a notice a week later that the site cannot be used for vehicle sales. Same mistake, one free call apart.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Signing a lease before checking zoning.**
- **Chasing the cheapest rent** over a compliant, visible site.
- **Underestimating signage costs and rules.**
- **Renting more space than your inventory needs** on day one.

## Tips

- Call zoning **first**, every time, in writing if you can.
- Match lot size to your inventory plan, not your ambition.
- Factor signage, deposit, and office setup into the true cost.

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the first thing to check on a location?

Zoning. Confirm the address allows used-vehicle sales before anything else.

### Does my lot need to be on a busy road?

Not required, but visibility is free marketing. Balance it against rent.

### Can I start with a small lot?

Yes — start lean and match the space to your inventory, then scale.

## Conclusion

The right dealership location is **legal first, profitable second**. Make the free zoning call before you sign, confirm the state's premises rules, then weigh visibility and rent against your budget. Get the order right and your lot becomes an asset; get it wrong and it becomes the most expensive mistake of your first year.
