Temporary tags let your buyer drive their new car legally while registration is processed — and issuing them is part of the paperwork you are responsible for on every sale. States track temp tags closely, so treating the stock like cash and keeping a clean per-deal file is what keeps you audit-ready. Here is how temp tags and the surrounding paperwork actually work.

## Quick answer

As a dealer you issue a **temporary tag** on each sale so the buyer can drive legally until registration. You log every tag, keep a complete deal file (title, odometer, tax, bill of sale), and retain records for the years your state requires.

## What temporary tags are for

When a customer buys a car, registration is not instant. A **temporary tag** bridges the gap, letting them drive legally in the meantime. As the dealer, you issue it — and you usually **log** each one, because states reconcile temp-tag stock to prevent abuse.

Treat temp tags like cash: account for every one.

## The paperwork on every sale

A complete deal file typically includes:

- The **title reassignment** to the buyer.
- An accurate **odometer disclosure** (federally required).
- The **bill of sale** and any required buyer's guide.
- **Sales tax** collected and recorded.
- The **temporary tag** issued and logged.

For more on the tax and title side, see our [sales tax and titling guide](/blog/sales-tax-and-titling-for-car-dealers).

## Build a per-deal system on day one

The dealers who breeze through audits all do the same simple thing: a **folder per deal**, digital or physical, with a short checklist of what goes in each. Five minutes per sale now saves you a reconstruction nightmare later. Set it up before your first sale, not after your first audit notice.

## A real example

Priya sells three cars in a week and figures she will "organize it later." An audit notice arrives, and later becomes a frantic weekend piecing together titles, disclosures, and temp-tag numbers from memory. Her friend Dana, who files each deal as it closes, pulls her records in five minutes. Same business, opposite stress levels.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Not logging temp tags.** States reconcile them.
- **Sloppy odometer disclosures.** A frequent violation.
- **Leaving records "for later."** Later is audit day.
- **Mixing collected sales tax with operating cash.**

## Tips

- Create a **deal folder template** and reuse it for every sale.
- Log temp tags as you issue them, not in a batch.
- Keep records for the full number of years your state requires.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who issues the temporary tag?

You do, as the dealer, usually logging each one for the state.

### How long do I keep deal records?

For the number of years your state specifies — often several. Confirm locally.

### What's the most-cited paperwork mistake?

Odometer disclosure errors and missing temp-tag logs are among the most common.

## Conclusion

Temporary tags and deal paperwork are not glamorous, but they are the backbone of a compliant dealership. Issue and log every temp tag, keep an exact odometer disclosure, and file a complete folder for every sale as it closes. Build that habit on day one and audits become a five-minute formality instead of a weekend of panic.
