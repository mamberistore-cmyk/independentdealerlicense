Garage liability insurance is the core policy most states require before they will license a car dealer. It covers bodily injury and property damage that come out of your dealership operations — a slip on the lot, a test-drive fender bender, a car you are moving that clips another. Here is what it actually covers, what it does not, and how to buy the right amount without overpaying.

## Quick answer

Garage liability protects you against injury and damage claims arising from running your lot. It is usually **required for licensing**, is **not** the same as your surety bond, and should be bought at or above your state's minimum limits — ideally with a small cushion.

## What garage liability covers

Garage liability is built specifically for automotive businesses. It typically responds to:

- A customer injured on your premises.
- Property damage caused by your operations.
- Incidents during test drives and moving inventory (confirm the wording).

A generic business liability policy usually **excludes** the auto operations that are your whole business — which is why the dealer-specific policy exists.

## It is not your surety bond

This trips up almost everyone: a **surety bond** protects your *customers and the state* if you commit fraud. **Insurance protects you.** You need both. For how bonds work, see our [cost breakdown](/blog/how-much-does-an-independent-dealer-license-cost), which covers bonds and premiums.

## What it does not cover

- **Your own inventory.** Damage to cars you own for sale needs **dealer's open lot / physical damage** coverage.
- **Customer cars in your care.** That is **garagekeepers** coverage, useful if you service or store vehicles.
- **Employee injuries.** That is **workers' compensation**, often required once you hire.

## Typical limits

Your state sets a **minimum limit** (often something like $50,000+ per occurrence, sometimes higher). Meet it — but consider going a little above, because one at-fault injury on a test drive can dwarf a lifetime of premiums.

## A real example

Priya buys the cheapest garage policy she can find. A customer trips on an uneven spot on her lot and is injured. The claim is covered — but her low limit is exhausted quickly, and she is exposed for the rest. A modestly higher limit would have cost a little more per month and saved her a lot of stress.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Buying generic business liability** that excludes auto operations.
- **Confusing insurance with your bond.** You need both.
- **Choosing the rock-bottom limit** to save a few dollars.
- **Ignoring exclusions.** Read when the policy will *not* respond.

## Tips

- Use a broker who writes **dealer** policies specifically.
- Get **three quotes** — rates vary a lot for identical coverage.
- Read the exclusions, not just the limits.
- Revisit coverage when you hire or grow inventory.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is garage liability required to get a dealer license?

In most states, yes — proof of it is part of the application.

### Is a surety bond the same as insurance?

No. The bond protects your customers and the state; insurance protects you. Both are required.

### What limit should I choose?

Meet your state's minimum, then consider a cushion above it. One serious claim can exceed the minimum quickly.

## Conclusion

Garage liability is the policy the state cares about most, because it protects the public from the risks of your operation. Buy the dealer-specific version, meet or beat the required limit, read the exclusions, and remember it is separate from your surety bond. Cover the basics properly and one bad day on the lot stays a headache instead of a catastrophe.
