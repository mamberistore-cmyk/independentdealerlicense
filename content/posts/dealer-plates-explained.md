Dealer plates let a licensed dealer legally drive and demonstrate inventory without registering each vehicle individually. They are attached to the business, not a single car, and they come with real rules about who can use them and when. Here is how dealer plates work, what they cost, and how to stay on the right side of the law.

## Quick answer

A dealer plate is a special license plate issued to your dealership that can be moved between vehicles you own for sale. It lets you road-test, demo, and transport inventory. You pay a per-plate fee, and misuse (like personal use) can put your license at risk.

## What dealer plates are for

Without dealer plates, you would have to register and de-register every car you touch. Dealer plates solve that by letting you legally operate inventory for **business purposes**: moving cars, test drives, dealer auctions, and customer demonstrations.

They are a privilege tied to your license — which is one more reason getting [properly licensed](/blog/how-to-get-independent-dealer-license) matters.

## Who can use a dealer plate (and when)

Rules vary by state, but the common theme is **business use only**:

- You, your employees, and customers on supervised test drives.
- Moving inventory to and from auctions, service, or another lot.

What is usually **not** allowed:

- Using a dealer plate on your personal daily driver.
- Letting family use a plated car for errands.
- Plating a vehicle that is not part of your for-sale inventory.

## What dealer plates cost

You typically pay a **per-plate fee** to your state, and most new dealers start with one or two plates. Budget roughly **$25 to $90 per plate**, sometimes with a small deposit. Order only what you need at first — you can add more as inventory grows. For the full startup math, see our [cost breakdown](/blog/how-much-does-an-independent-dealer-license-cost).

## A real example

Dana starts her lot with two dealer plates. She uses one to move cars between the auction and her lot, and swaps the other onto whichever car a customer wants to test drive. When her brother asks to borrow a plated car for a weekend trip, she says no — that personal use could jeopardize her license. Small discipline, big protection.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Personal use.** The fastest way to draw a violation.
- **Plating non-inventory vehicles.** Plates are for cars you are actually selling.
- **Ignoring insurance.** A plated car on the road still needs proper dealer coverage.

## Tips

- Keep a simple log of which plate is on which car and why.
- Confirm your insurance covers plated vehicles and permissive drivers.
- Start with one or two plates; scale as your inventory does.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I drive a dealer-plated car personally?

Generally no. Dealer plates are for business use — test drives, demos, and moving inventory, not personal transportation.

### How many dealer plates can I get?

It varies by state and often scales with your sales volume. Start with what you need.

### Do plated cars need insurance?

Yes. A dealer-plated vehicle on public roads still requires appropriate dealer/garage coverage.

## Conclusion

Dealer plates are a genuine convenience — they let you run inventory like a real business instead of registering every car. Just remember they are a **business tool tied to your license**: keep them on for-sale inventory, avoid personal use, and make sure your insurance follows the plate. Do that, and they will save you time and paperwork for years.
