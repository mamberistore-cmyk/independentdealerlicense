Yes, you can almost always get a dealer surety bond even with bad credit — you will just pay a higher premium, and sometimes post collateral. Very few people are truly un-bondable. Here is how bond pricing works, what a high-risk bond costs, and how to lower your rate as your credit recovers.

## Quick answer

Bad credit does not usually block you from a bond; it raises the **premium** (the small percentage of the bond amount you pay each year). Specialty "high-risk" sureties exist. Get licensed, operate cleanly, and refinance the bond at a better rate later.

## How bond pricing works

Your state sets the **bond amount** (often $10,000–$50,000). You do not pay that — you pay a **premium**, a percentage of it, set mostly by your **credit**:

- **Strong credit:** roughly 1–3%.
- **Weaker credit:** 5–10%+, sometimes with collateral.

So on a $25,000 bond, great credit might cost about $375/year, while rough credit might cost $2,000+ — same bond, different premium. Our [cost breakdown](/blog/how-much-does-an-independent-dealer-license-cost) covers how this fits your budget.

## Getting bonded with bad credit

Specialty sureties write **high-risk** bonds for exactly this situation. Expect:

- A **higher premium** than a top-tier applicant.
- Possibly a **collateral** requirement.
- Standard underwriting questions — answer them honestly.

The key point: a low credit score raises your cost, but it rarely makes you un-bondable.

## How to lower your rate over time

1. **Clean up your credit** — pay down balances and fix report errors.
2. **Operate cleanly** — no bond claims keeps you attractive to sureties.
3. **Shop at renewal** — quotes vary, so re-price each year.
4. **Refinance the bond** once your credit improves.

## A real example

Marco has a rough credit history and pays 8% on his $25,000 bond in year one — about $2,000. He fixes two report errors, pays down a card, and never has a claim. At renewal, a new surety offers him 4%, cutting his premium in half. Same coverage, better price, earned by clean operation.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Assuming bad credit disqualifies you.** It usually doesn't.
- **Taking the first quote.** Rates vary between sureties.
- **Misrepresenting your history** to shave a point — it can void the bond.
- **Generating a claim.** It makes your next bond far more expensive.

## Tips

- Get a few quotes, including from high-risk specialists.
- Fix obvious credit-report errors before applying.
- Operate claim-free and re-price the bond every year.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get a dealer bond with bad credit?

Almost always, yes — at a higher premium, and sometimes with collateral.

### Will I ever pay the full bond amount?

No. You pay a premium (a percentage) unless a claim is paid — and then the surety recovers it from you.

### Can my rate go down later?

Yes. Improve your credit, stay claim-free, and refinance the bond at renewal.

## Conclusion

Bad credit is a cost problem, not a wall. You can get bonded, get licensed, and start selling — then chip your premium down by fixing your credit, operating cleanly, and shopping at renewal. Do not let a credit score stop you from starting; let a clean track record make your bond cheaper every year.
