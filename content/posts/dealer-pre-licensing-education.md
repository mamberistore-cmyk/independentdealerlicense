Many states require a **pre-licensing course** before they will issue a dealer license. It is usually a few hours online or in a classroom, covers titling, disclosures, and recordkeeping, and often ends with a short exam. It can feel like a formality, but the lessons it teaches are exactly the ones that get careless dealers fined. Here is what to expect and how to pass.

## Quick answer

Where required, dealer pre-licensing is a **4–8 hour course** (online or in person) costing about **$100–$300**, covering the rules you will actually use. Take notes, focus on titling and odometer rules, and the exam is very passable.

## What the course covers

Curricula vary by state, but most include:

- **Title reassignment** and transfer rules.
- **Odometer disclosure** requirements (federally regulated).
- **Advertising** and consumer-protection basics.
- **Temporary tags** and dealer plates.
- **Recordkeeping** — what to keep and for how long.

These are not trivia. They are the exact areas where mistakes turn into violations, so treat the course as job training, not a hoop.

## How long and how much

Expect **4 to 8 hours** and a fee of roughly **$100 to $300**, one time. It fits neatly alongside the rest of [getting licensed](/blog/how-to-get-independent-dealer-license) — you can take the course while you shop for a bond and hunt for a location.

## How to pass the exam

The exam is usually straightforward if you actually engage with the material:

1. **Take notes** on titling, odometer, and recordkeeping — these show up most.
2. **Learn the "why."** Rules stick better when you understand the consumer-protection reason.
3. **Do any practice questions** the course offers.
4. **Keep your completion certificate** somewhere safe — you must show it.

## A real example

Sam rushes the course, half-watching while doing other things. He passes the exam but misses the odometer rules — and months later fumbles a disclosure that becomes a violation. Dana, who took notes, breezes through both the exam and her first year of paperwork. Same course, very different outcomes.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Treating it as a formality.** The paperwork rules are real money.
- **Losing the completion certificate.** No certificate, no license.
- **Cramming and forgetting** the titling and odometer rules you will use weekly.

## Tips

- Keep the certificate with your license documents.
- Focus study time on **titling, odometer, and records**.
- Take the course early so it does not delay your application.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is pre-licensing education required everywhere?

No — it depends on your state and sometimes your dealer type. Check your state's requirements.

### How hard is the exam?

Not hard if you engage with the material. It tests practical rules, not trick questions.

### How long is the certificate valid?

It varies by state; keep it with your application documents and confirm timing locally.

## Conclusion

If your state requires pre-licensing education, do not treat it as a box to check. The few hours it takes teach the **titling, odometer, and recordkeeping** rules that separate dealers who sail through audits from those who get fined. Take notes, understand the "why," keep your certificate, and you will pass the exam and your first real audit alike.
