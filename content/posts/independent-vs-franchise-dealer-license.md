If you are deciding how to sell cars legally, the first fork in the road is this: an **independent (used) dealer license** lets you buy and sell pre-owned vehicles from any source, while a **franchise (new) dealer license** ties you to a specific manufacturer to sell their new cars. Most people starting a small lot want the independent license — it is cheaper, faster, and does not require a factory agreement.

Below is a plain-English comparison so you can pick the right path before you spend a dollar.

## Quick answer

- **Independent dealer license:** sell used vehicles, no manufacturer franchise, lower barrier to entry.
- **Franchise dealer license:** sell new vehicles under a brand (Toyota, Ford, etc.), requires a factory franchise agreement, far more capital and oversight.

If you plan to flip used cars or run a used-car lot, you want the independent license.

## What each license actually allows

A **franchise dealer** has a contract with a manufacturer and sells that brand's new inventory, plus used cars taken in on trade. Getting a franchise is a business deal with the automaker — they control location, facility standards, and inventory.

An **independent dealer** has no such contract. You source inventory wherever you can: dealer-only auctions, trade-ins, private sellers, and other dealers. You can sell almost any used make and model. This flexibility is exactly why most first-time dealers choose it.

## How the requirements differ

Both licenses share a common base — a business entity, a surety bond, insurance, a compliant location, and honest recordkeeping. The differences are in scale.

### Location and facility

Franchise dealers must meet the **manufacturer's** facility standards (showroom size, service bays, signage), which are expensive. Independent dealers only need to meet the **state's** location rules, which are far more modest.

### Capital

A franchise store is a multi-million-dollar operation. An independent lot can start in the low-to-mid five figures. For a full breakdown of independent costs, see our guide on [how much a dealer license costs](/blog/how-much-does-an-independent-dealer-license-cost).

### The franchise agreement

This is the big one: you cannot simply "apply" for a franchise license. You must be awarded a franchise by the automaker, which is rare and highly competitive. The independent license has no gatekeeper beyond your state's requirements.

## A real example

Two friends, Sam and Dana, both want to sell cars. Sam wants to open a Honda store; Dana wants a small used-car lot.

Sam quickly learns Honda is not handing out new franchises in his area, and even if they were, the buy-in and facility requirements are beyond his budget. Dana, meanwhile, follows her state's [step-by-step licensing process](/blog/how-to-get-independent-dealer-license), posts a surety bond, and is selling used cars within a couple of months. Same goal, very different roads.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Assuming "dealer license" means one thing.** Search your state's site for the *used* or *independent* license specifically.
- **Chasing a franchise with too little capital.** If you are bootstrapping, the independent route is almost always the realistic one.
- **Ignoring your state's used-dealer rules** because you read about franchise requirements online — they are different programs.

## Tips

- Decide your inventory strategy first (used vs. new), then match the license to it.
- Call your state motor vehicle department and ask specifically about the **independent/used** dealer license.
- Budget for the whole operation — bond, insurance, location — not just the license fee.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an independent dealer sell new cars?

Generally no. Selling brand-new vehicles from a manufacturer requires that manufacturer's franchise. Independent dealers sell used inventory.

### Is an independent license easier to get?

Yes. It has no franchise gatekeeper and lower facility and capital requirements, though you still must meet your state's bond, insurance, and location rules.

### Can I switch from independent to franchise later?

You can pursue a franchise later, but it is a separate business relationship with an automaker, not an upgrade to your existing license.

## Conclusion

For almost everyone starting out, the **independent dealer license** is the practical choice: it lets you sell used vehicles from any source without a factory agreement, at a fraction of the cost and complexity of a franchise. Confirm your state's exact used-dealer requirements, budget for the full operation, and you will be selling far sooner than the franchise route would ever allow.
