After years of answering the same questions — first at a licensing window, then from behind my own lot — I've noticed the same ten come up again and again. So here they are, with straight answers. No fluff, no upsell. If you're just starting to research a dealer license, this is the fastest way to get your bearings.

## 1. How many cars can I sell before I need a license?

Every state sets a threshold, usually somewhere around **3 to 6 vehicles in a 12-month period,** though it varies. But the number is only half the story. The other half is **intent.** If you're buying vehicles specifically to resell them for profit, you can be considered a dealer even below the count. Selling your own personal cars occasionally? Fine. Flipping cars as a side income? You're a dealer — get licensed.

## 2. How much does a dealer license cost?

The *license fee* itself is usually **$100 to $500.** But the real cost of getting started — bond premium, insurance, a compliant location, signage — typically lands in the **low-to-mid four figures** before you buy any inventory. Don't confuse the application fee with the cost of the business. Budget for the whole thing. (I've got a full cost breakdown if you want every line item.)

## 3. Do I really need a physical lot? Can't I run it from home?

In most states, a **retail** used-car license requires a **commercial, properly zoned location** with signage and often posted hours. Your kitchen table won't cut it. *However,* some states offer lighter options — **wholesale-only** licenses (selling only to other dealers) or limited **home-based** licenses for certain dealer types — with reduced location requirements. Check your specific state before assuming you need a full lot.

## 4. What's a surety bond, and is it insurance?

No — and this is the most common confusion I see. A **surety bond** is a financial guarantee that protects your *customers and the state* if you commit fraud or don't pay what you owe. **Insurance protects you; the bond protects them.** You pay a **premium** (a small percentage of the bond amount) based mostly on your credit. And if a claim gets paid, the surety comes after *you* to recover it — so the bond is not a slush fund. Operate clean and it's just a cheap annual line item.

## 5. Can I get a license with bad credit?

Yes, in almost all cases. Your credit mostly affects your **bond premium** — bad credit means a higher percentage, and sometimes a collateral deposit, but very few people are truly un-bondable. There are sureties that specialize in higher-risk applicants. Get licensed, operate cleanly, and refinance the bond at a better rate once your credit recovers.

## 6. Can I get a license with something on my criminal record?

Often, yes. States run background checks, but a single old blemish is frequently *not* a dealbreaker — it depends on the offense and your state's rules. The thing that reliably gets people *denied* isn't the record itself; it's **hiding it.** Disclose everything on the application. If you're worried about a specific item, call the licensing office and ask how they handle it. Honesty almost always beats concealment here.

## 7. How long does the whole process take?

If you hustle and your state isn't backed up, **four to eight weeks** is realistic. The slow parts are almost always **finding and securing a compliant location** and waiting in the **site-inspection queue.** The paperwork itself is a few evenings of work. Doing the steps *in parallel* — class, bond, and location hunt all at once — is the single best way to compress the timeline.

## 8. What can I actually do with the license?

A lot more than "sell cars legally." Your license lets you:

- Buy at **dealer-only auctions** (where the cheap inventory lives).
- Get **dealer plates** to move and demo inventory without registering each car.
- Issue **temporary tags** and handle titling for customers.
- Operate as a legitimate business that customers actually trust.

For most people, the **auction access alone** justifies the whole cost.

## 9. What insurance do I need?

At minimum, **garage liability insurance** — most states require proof of it to license you, and it covers injuries and damage from your operations. You'll also want **dealer plate/drive-away** coverage confirmed in writing, and if you carry real inventory value, **dealer's open lot** (physical damage) coverage to protect your own cars from theft, fire, and hail. Add **garagekeepers** if you hold or service customer vehicles, and **workers' comp** the moment you hire. Get three quotes — rates vary a lot.

## 10. What's the fastest way to lose my license?

A few reliable ways, all avoidable:

- **Letting your bond or insurance lapse** (they're conditions of licensure — lapse and you're operating illegally).
- **Sloppy or false odometer disclosures** (federally regulated, taken very seriously).
- **Bad or missing records** when an audit comes.
- **Fraud or misrepresentation** to customers or the state.

Every one of these comes down to the same thing: keep your paperwork clean, your renewals current, and your dealings honest. Do that, and the license basically takes care of itself.
