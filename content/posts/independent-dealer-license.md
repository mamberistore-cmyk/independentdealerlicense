# Do You Need An Independent Dealer License?

Getting an independent dealer license is one of those things that seems easy until you open out the application. Then there are the business address problems, zoning laws, insurance, paperwork, taxes, inspections and a mound of things no one mentions in the sales pitch.

The good news is that it can be done if you approach the operation as a checklist and not a mystery. The important point is that there is no national " independent dealer license " that is uniform . Licensing is typically regulated by the state in the U.S., sometimes under the influence of county or municipal legislation. Costs and license requirements depend on your sort of company and locality, says the Small company Administration in the United States.

So are you going to buy a few of cars at auction and flip them or are you going to start a complete dealership with inventory and financing and a lot? Your answer changes what you need to do.

This manual explains the method in clear English, with an emphasis on the practical matters that matter most.

## What does it mean to have an independent dealer licence? ### The question
The real problem isn’t whether you sell online or only once in a while. It’s your state’s definition of dealer activity and any exceptions that may apply.” If you are not sure, check with the licensing agency before you design a business model based on an assumption.

An independent dealer is often a vehicle seller that is not under a manufacturer's franchise system. The license name is different. One state may call it a used motor vehicle dealer license, another a 'independent dealer,' and another yet may classify licenses by car type or sales activity.

That difference matters, since looking for a “independent dealer license” can lead you to the wrong government website. Contact the state motor vehicle department, where the dealership will be located. Then check with the city or county for zoning and business-location requirements.

The SBA stresses that license and permit requirements vary based on the kind of company, its location and government restrictions. To summarize, a universal national application does not exist.

## Step 1. Choose Your Business Structure.

Think about how the firm will be structured legally before you apply. This may be a sole proprietorship, LLC, corporation, or any other kind of structure authorized in your state, depending on your situation.

The SBA says your company structure affects taxes, paperwork, financing and personal responsibility. You’ll need to choose this option before you start filling out dealer paperwork, since the names and documents on your application must match your legal company records.

Many small companies choose LLCs because they give a formal corporate structure and separate business from personal matters, but the best choice for you will depend on your particular situation. A tax consultant and a corporate attorney can help you review your options.

## Step 2: Business registration and ID acquisition

After you’ve figured up the structure and name, register the appropriate registrations your state requires.

This might include, as appropriate to the structure and circumstances:

- Set up your company with the state- Trade or dba name registration.
- Employer Identification Number (EIN).
- Registration for state tax.
- Establishing a company locally.
- Sales taxes registration, if it applies.

The SBA states that an EIN is used for important business activities such as taxes, recruiting employees, and opening a business bank account. The SBA’s EIN application is free.

An EIN is not the same as a dealer license. They aren't the same things. The former defines the business for federal tax reasons and the latter provides for controlled automobile sales under applicable law.

## Step 4: Get insurance and financial prerequisites in place.

Insurance needs vary by state, by kind of license, and by firm structure. Most dealer applications need proof of certain coverages prior to being licensed.

Depending on where you are, you could discover restrictions such as:

- Dealer Liability Insurance- Garage Liability- surety bonds
- Insurance for Business
- Evidence of financial responsibility.- Licensing costs or application fees

Do not guess the required limits. Use current application material or contact the licensing agency.

One typical error is to get an insurance coverage that looks good but is not sufficient to satisfy the dealer-license requirement. Tell the insurer what license you're looking for and what the agency needs.

## FAQ ## 1. Are independent sellers licensed nationally?

No. Dealer licensing is typically a state issue, although there may be other regulations at the municipal level. The SBA advises businesses to look at the rules for their activity and location.

### 2: Should I Buy From a Real Dealership?

This varies by state and by license type. Some agencies have rigid geographical criteria, others may accept other firm structures. •Always check the current state regulations before signing a lease. #3: How much does it cost to get an independent dealer license?

There is no fixed pricing. Total startup cost may include application charges, licensing fees, bonds, insurance, signage, inspections, and municipal permits.

### 4. How long does it take to get approved?

Timing is quite variable. Authorized applications for the site and proper documentation may be handled much quicker than applications requiring modifications, scheduling of inspections or zoning clarification.

## 5: Can I sell cars online without a dealer license?

Selling online isn't always a way around dealer license laws. The worry is usually your corporate activity and jurisdiction, not whether the consumer sees the automobile in person or not.

### 6: What is the Buyers Guide from the FTC?

The Buyers Guide is a disclosure required by the FTC’s Used Car Rule for dealerships to whom the rule applies. It gives buyers with important information on warranty coverage and disclosures about the condition of the automobile (### 7). Where should I start?
