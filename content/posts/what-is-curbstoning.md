Curbstoning is selling cars for profit without a dealer license — often by parking cars along a curb or a lot you do not own, with a phone number in the window. It is illegal in every state, enforcement has gotten sharper, and the penalties usually cost far more than simply getting licensed. Here is what curbstoning is, how it is caught, and why it is not worth the risk.

## Quick answer

Curbstoning = selling vehicles for profit without the required dealer license. States detect it through online listing patterns, and penalties range from steep fines to criminal charges for repeat offenders. Getting [licensed](/blog/how-to-get-independent-dealer-license) is cheaper and safer.

## Where the name comes from

The classic move is parking cars along a **curb** or in a lot the seller does not control, with a handwritten phone number in the window. The seller looks like a private individual but is really running an unlicensed business.

## How states detect it

Enforcement is smarter than it used to be. Investigators trawl online marketplaces looking for the tells:

- The **same phone number** attached to many listings.
- The same "private seller" moving car after car.
- Titles that never rest in the seller's name for long.

If your activity fits that pattern, you look like an unlicensed dealer — even if you call yourself a hobbyist.

## The penalties

Curbstoning is taken seriously. Consequences can include:

- **Fines** that dwarf the cost of a license.
- **Vehicle and advertising seizures** in some jurisdictions.
- **Criminal charges** for repeat offenders.
- A closed door to ever operating legitimately in the business.

## A real example

Ben flips five cars in a few months using one phone number across all his listings. An investigator spots the pattern in an afternoon. Ben's "side hustle" turns into fines and a warning that another sale means bigger trouble. The [license he skipped](/blog/how-much-does-an-independent-dealer-license-cost) would have cost less and unlocked cheaper auction inventory too.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Assuming "private seller" hides a business.** Patterns give it away.
- **Reusing one phone number** across many listings.
- **Believing enforcement is rare.** Online detection has changed that.

## Tips

- If you are buying to resell for profit, get a license first.
- Understand your state's threshold for "dealer" activity.
- The license's auction access alone often outweighs its cost.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is curbstoning actually illegal?

Yes, in every state. Selling vehicles for profit without a license is unlicensed dealing.

### How do they catch curbstoners?

Mostly by spotting repeated listings tied to one seller or phone number on online marketplaces.

### What's the penalty?

Fines that typically exceed the license cost, and criminal charges for repeat offenders.

## Conclusion

Curbstoning might look like an easy shortcut, but it is illegal, increasingly easy to detect, and expensive when caught. The honest path — getting a dealer license — costs less than a single penalty, unlocks dealer auctions, and lets you build something real instead of hiding from investigators. If profit is the point, get licensed and sell with confidence.
