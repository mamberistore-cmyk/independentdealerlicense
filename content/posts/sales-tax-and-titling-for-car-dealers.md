Selling the car is the fun part; the paperwork is what keeps your license. As a dealer, you are responsible for **collecting sales tax**, **transferring the title** correctly, and **issuing temporary tags** on each sale. Get this right and audits are boring. Get it wrong and it can cost you money — or your license. Here is the plain-English version.

## Quick answer

On each retail sale you typically: collect the correct sales tax, complete an accurate title transfer with an odometer disclosure, remit the tax to your state on schedule, and issue a temporary tag so the buyer can drive legally until registration.

## Collecting sales tax

Every state that has a sales tax expects dealers to **collect it at the point of sale** and remit it to the state on a set schedule (monthly or quarterly). A few things to know:

- The **rate** and **filing frequency** vary by state and sometimes locality.
- Out-of-state buyers can complicate things — confirm your state's rule.
- Keep tax collected **separate** in your mind (and ideally your accounting) — it is not your money.

You will register for a **sales tax permit** as part of getting set up. See our [step-by-step licensing guide](/blog/how-to-get-independent-dealer-license) for where this fits.

## Transferring the title

The title transfer is the legal handoff of ownership. On each sale you generally:

1. Complete the **title reassignment** to the buyer.
2. Record an accurate **odometer disclosure** (federally required and taken seriously).
3. Provide any state-required bill of sale and buyer's guide.
4. Submit the paperwork so the buyer can register the vehicle.

### Odometer disclosure

This deserves its own line: record the **exact mileage** and mark the correct status. "I forgot" is not a defense, and errors here turn paperwork into a violation fast.

## Temporary tags

Most states let dealers issue a **temporary tag** so the buyer can drive the car legally while registration is processed. You will usually log each temp tag you issue. Treat the temp-tag stock like cash — states track it.

## A real example

Marco sells a sedan and collects the right sales tax, but scribbles the odometer reading and skips the temp-tag log. Months later an audit flags both. The sale was fine — the **paperwork** was the problem. A five-minute-per-deal habit would have avoided it entirely.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Sloppy odometer disclosures.** Federal issue — always exact.
- **Spending collected sales tax.** It belongs to the state.
- **Missing temp-tag logs.** States reconcile these.
- **Late tax filings.** Set calendar reminders.

## Tips

- Set up a **folder per deal** on day one — title, disclosure, tax, temp tag.
- Put tax-filing dates on a recurring calendar reminder.
- When unsure about an out-of-state sale, ask your state revenue department.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I collect sales tax on every sale?

In sales-tax states, yes, on retail sales — with specific rules for out-of-state buyers. Confirm your state's requirements.

### Is the odometer disclosure really that important?

Yes. It is federally regulated and one of the most common sources of dealer violations.

### Who issues the temporary tag?

You do, as the dealer, usually logging each one for the state.

## Conclusion

The paperwork side of dealing — **sales tax, titling, and temp tags** — is where careful dealers separate themselves from the ones who get fined. Build a simple per-deal system, record odometers exactly, keep tax money set aside, and file on time. It is boring, and boring is exactly what you want when the auditor calls.
